## Stack

## Users

The primary visitor is a parent or a gamer deciding whether to trust a stranger with $700–$1,500
instead of buying a boxed prebuilt from a big-box store. They arrive uncertain, evaluating
competence and accountability rather than shopping a spec sheet. The purchaser and the end user are
often different people (parent buying, kid using).

## Product Purpose

Zayd's Custom PCs sells custom-built PCs and the services around them in Orange County, CA.

- **Services:** custom PC builds · repairs · upgrades · PC maintenance subscriptions
- **Workshops:** Build Your Own PC · Cybersecurity Awareness

Success is a single action: the visitor submits the quote form. That form is the sole conversion
goal of the entire site; every section must be able to reach it.

The form branches on its first question and is the intake for **all five paths** — new build,
repair, upgrade, maintenance plan, and workshop. Only the branch the visitor chose is enabled;
the rest are disabled so no submission carries another path's blank fields. Repairs deliberately
ask no budget (diagnosis comes first). The workshop path **registers interest and never implies a
booking**, because dates are still TBD; its confirmation must keep saying so until they are set.

## Positioning

Every build is quoted individually — there are no fixed prebuilt tiers and no standing parts lists.
The differentiator is part-level transparency: the customer is told exactly which parts went in and
gets the receipt. A local human answers the phone.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Custom builds start at **$700**. This figure must be visible in the first viewport.
- No fixed prebuilt tiers, no standing parts lists — these are not products and must never be shown
  as though they were.
- The decision range the site is written for is roughly $700–$1,500.
- Repairs start at **$45**. Upgrades start at **$45**. Maintenance plans start at **$35/month**.
  Supplied by the client 2026-08-18; these are floors, not fixed prices — the quote still follows
  diagnosis.
- Undecided / not supplied: workshop dates and pricing, turnaround times. These are open and must
  not be invented.

## Brand Commitments

- Name and identity: **Zayd's Custom PCs**; existing logo in two SVG lockups (16:9 and square) that
  must sit natively in any visual world rather than look pasted on.
- The site's argument is that a quoted build beats a boxed prebuilt. That argument is made from
  structural facts only: part-level transparency, no bloatware, no proprietary parts or PSUs,
  upgradeable later, individual part warranties, aesthetics, and a local human who answers the
  phone.
- Binding prohibitions in that argument: never a named retailer, never a competitor's price, never a
  benchmark number, never a fabricated side-by-side.
- Binding visual prohibitions: never carbon fiber, never glassmorphism, never cyan-on-purple gamer
  chrome, never stock photography.
- Register: competent and accountable, not slick. The reference image the user gave is a feel
  reference only, never a content reference.

## Product Principles

1. Accountability is the product. Show the itemized reality; never claim a result that cannot be
   shown.
2. Every build is quoted. Nothing on the site may harden into a fixed tier or a standing parts list.
3. One action. Everything routes to the quote form; no competing conversion.
4. Only first-party evidence. Real photography of real builds, labelled with nothing beyond the
   CPU/GPU.
5. Argue structurally, never comparatively. The case against a boxed prebuilt is made from
   properties, not from another company's name, price, or numbers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

scrolling on mobile.
